01 · Definition

What Nightshade is, and the gap it closes

Striking high-value targets across operational depth requires three properties at once: terminal precision, high cruise speed, and expendable economics. Slow loitering munitions fly at 100–200 km/h. That sluggish profile leaves them vulnerable to radar-directed cannon, short-range surface-to-air missiles, and tactical radio jammers. Traditional cruise missiles survive and hit reliably, but cost ₹20–35 crore per round. At that price, armed forces cannot fire salvos without exhausting inventories within days.

Nightshade resolves this contradiction. A compact turbojet propels the airframe at 550 km/h in cruise and 700 km/h in terminal dive, shrinking the defender's engagement window to seconds. When satellite signals fail under hostile jamming, an onboard four-tier navigation stack maintains track until the dual-band imaging seeker acquires the target silhouette. Priced at roughly ₹1.5 crore (≈$150,000), Nightshade turns high-speed strike into an attritable munition. It is the first platform family to run the Apollyon shared core.

04 · Roles

One airframe, three roles, several missions

The same airframe leaves the line as a one-way effector, a live-fire training target, or a decoy. The warhead bay decides which; nothing else changes.

Mission 01

Precision strike

Autonomous strikes on static and relocatable tactical assets without continuous data links. The dual-band seeker locks onto target features independently of satellite signals, destroying command posts, fuel dumps, revetted aircraft, and ammunition hubs.

Mission 02

SEAD / counter-radar

A passive anti-radiation seeker homes directly onto enemy radar emissions. By striking surveillance radars, missile fire-control units, and high-power jammers, Nightshade punches corridors through integrated air defence networks without risking crewed aircraft.

Mission 03

Maritime interdiction

Configured with maritime contrast tracking, the effector engages corvettes, missile boats, and logistics vessels in contested littoral waters. High terminal velocity overwhelms close-in weapon systems while keeping surface combatants outside defensive perimeters.

Mission 04 · revenue

Air-defence training target

The target-drone configuration replaces the warhead with Luneberg lenses, providing air defence crews with a realistic 700 km/h target. Live-fire training consumes units on recurring operational budgets. See target-drone details below.

Mission 05

Decoy

With radar augmentation in place of the warhead, the same airframe presents a credible strike signature and pulls interceptors and radar attention away from the attacking package. It launches from the same rail, is flown by the same crew, and runs the same flight software.

05 · Navigation

Four layers, each a fallback for the last

The navigation stack is the primary physical inheritance from the shared core architecture. Each layer acts as an autonomous fallback for the one preceding it, ensuring the round reaches its target area even through dense electronic warfare.

Layer-by-layer
LayerFunctionBehaviour under attack
1 · CRPA antennaControlled reception pattern array with multi-beam adaptive null-steering.Steers adaptive spatial nulls toward ground jammers before unwanted radio energy reaches the receiver front end.
2 · Multiband GNSSConcurrent L1, L2, and L5 tracking across GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and NavIC.Maintains primary geodetic position while the CRPA suppresses interference.
3 · Tactical INSTactical-grade MEMS inertial bridge with thermal compensation.Maintains attitude and velocity dead-reckoning during satellite blackouts; drift accumulates purely by elapsed time.
4 · Terminal seekerTonbo TRAP-1 dual-band electro-optical and infrared imaging sensor.Locks onto physical target geometry during the final dive; operates completely independently of radio frequencies.

Sensor Suite Design Note: Active altimeters (radar and laser) are deliberately excluded from the Nightshade sensor suite. Active emissions expose the munition's ingress vector to hostile electronic support measures (ESM), while radar altimeters add unacceptable SWaP-C overhead to an attritable platform. Instead, Nightshade operates in total electromagnetic silence: vertical channel control relies on barometric altitude fused with inertial state, while midcourse navigation correlates passive optical/IR ground snapshots with pre-loaded digital elevation models to arrest drift. 06 · Terminal

The Tonbo TRAP-1 seeker

A strike munition depends fundamentally on its terminal seeker, yet high-resolution sensors are frequently throttled by foreign export regimes. Apollyon integrates the TRAP-1 dual-band EO/IR sensor through an engineering partnership with Tonbo Imaging in Bengaluru. This domestic partnership anchors focal-plane array manufacturing and optical assemblies inside Indian industrial base.

The TRAP-1 combines uncooled long-wave infrared with visible-spectrum optics. Onboard compute models run contrast tracking and edge matching against stored target silhouettes, maintaining lock through thermal smoke, camouflage, and twilight transitions. Because the terminal guidance loop operates purely on passive physics, hostile ground jammers have no radio signal to interrupt. Seeker subsystem →

07 · Launch

Ground-mobile, with a validated launch record

Nightshade Mk II deploys from a compact pneumatic catapult or directly from a light vehicle rail. Two operators erect, fuel, and initialize the system in under 15 minutes without heavy engineering machinery. In early 2026, Apollyon demonstrated this mobile deployment doctrine by executing the first car-launch of a jet-powered UAV on Indian soil. The long-range Mk III variant incorporates a solid-propellant rocket booster for zero-length canister launches from unimproved frontline positions. Detailed launch mechanics appear in launch and ground systems.

08 · Variant

The target-drone variant, and why it matters commercially

The Mk II target drone uses the identical composite airframe, turbojet engine, and autopilot hardware as the combat effector. Apollyon replaces the 15 kg explosive payload with passive Luneberg lenses and active radar transponders. Flying at 700 km/h, the airframe simulates hostile anti-ship missiles and attack aircraft for live-fire training by naval and ground-based air defence units.

This variant serves three commercial and industrial objectives:

  • Sustained revenue: Armed forces buy target drones through operational expenditure (OpEx) budgets. These funds recur annually and bypass the multi-year procurement delays typical of capital weapons acquisition.
  • Live flight validation: Peacetime air-defence exercises generate dozens of live flights across varied operational climates, refining flight control algorithms and engine reliability on customer funding.
  • Immediate export access: Because the target drone is an unarmed training asset, export clearance requires fewer regulatory hurdles than offensive strike weapons, enabling early bilateral sales in friendly markets.
  • Order book: A pilot purchase order is signed with Tonbo Imaging for the Nightshade platform, covering the target-drone configuration through 2026. Field trials and certification follow in 2027, ahead of one-way effector mass production.
