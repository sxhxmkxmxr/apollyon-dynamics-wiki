Apollyon Cortex the layer in between

Tactical formations receive uncoordinated feeds from ISR drones, ground radars, vehicle telemetry, and field reports across disparate networks. Cortex unifies these inputs into a common operational picture, executing edge change detection and role-adapted dissemination without external cloud dependencies.

02 · Use cases

What it is used for

Modern battlefields generate overwhelming sensor volumes. The operational bottleneck is the absence of an edge-tier software engine that normalizes multi-sensor telemetry, maintains historical memory, and pushes role-tailored intelligence across bandwidth-constrained tactical bearers.

03 · Specifics

The intelligence pipeline

Cortex runs five stages in sequence. Each one narrows the problem, and each one is auditable on its own.

Intelligence layer · sequence
StageQuestion answered
01 · DetectWhat objects and classes are present in the feed?
02 · AnalyseWhat activity and behaviour are those objects exhibiting?
03 · AssessWhat is the damage and condition state?
04 · FlagIs this a meaningful change against the stored history of this ground?
05 · EscalateWhich operator reviews it, and with what evidence attached?
04 · Specifics

Delivery logic and precedence

Sensor fusion solves only half the tactical problem. The greater challenge is formatting and routing actionable intelligence across contested communication bearers. Before transmitting any update, Cortex evaluates three deterministic gating criteria:

  1. Authorization: Role-based cryptographic access control confirms whether the recipient node is permitted to view the underlying classification tier.
  2. Mission relevance: The world-graph engine filters updates against the node's geographic sector, operational role, and immediate tactical tasking.
  3. Channel capacity: Cortex measures real-time link latency and bandwidth, automatically downsampling full evidence dossiers into lightweight vector alerts over VHF or tactical radio.

05 · Worked example

One event, four products

One hazard on Route R-17 — a partial bridge collapse on the east span, detected by EO/ISR, assessed once — composed into the form each authorised user can act on:

  • Analyst evidence package: Baseline and current imagery side by side, a written assessment, review state and reviewer.
  • MDCC operator alert: Status, impact, impact detail, next action, valid-until time.
  • Mounted-user route warning: Hazard, alternate route, detour cost in distance and minutes, issue time.
  • Higher-headquarters report: Location, grid, time, impact, affected units, summary, recommendation, approval chain.
06 · Specifics

Processing, memory and integrity

All neural inference, tracking, and graph compilation execute locally on edge compute. The system needs no reachback to external data centers or satellite cloud uplinks, so operations continue when long-haul communications are severed.

  • Memory: Geographic spatial cells maintain persistent historical state across successive ISR passes, transforming raw video streams into automated change-detection networks.
  • Integrity: End-to-end encryption secures data at rest and in transit, governed by hardware-rooted device credentials and role-based clearance.
  • Provenance: Every track and assessed event carries origin metadata, statistical confidence bounds, and sensor modality tags. Position quality is explicitly marked as verified, estimated, stale, or conflicting rather than being silently averaged.
  • Auditing: Every operator interaction and raw sensor frame records to a tamper-resistant timeline, enabling post-mission playback and evidence verification.
07 · Boundaries

What Cortex explicitly does not do

Stated exclusions

No autonomous engagement. No weapon control. No IFF. No higher-headquarters C2. Cortex assesses and disseminates; the decision to engage stays with the chain of command, and the system is specified so that it cannot quietly acquire that authority.

This boundary is deliberate and is consistent with the company's position on discriminate force. A system that fuses sensors and also fires weapons removes the human review step precisely where discrimination is decided.

Cortex is deployed on the Mobile Distributed Command Centre as its primary tactical host, and runs unchanged in an operations room, at a fixed command post, on a rugged edge server or at higher headquarters.

Architecture

Where this sits in the stack

Cortex is the portfolio's data layer, not a vehicle stack: it ingests sensors, builds a world graph, and compiles role-adapted outputs. The vehicle side of the architecture — hardware, software, the flight loop and navigation under denial — is on the shared core; the Cortex pipeline itself is Fig. 07 there.
