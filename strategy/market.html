01 · Addressable market

The segments we sell into

Layered air defence networks and widespread electronic warfare restrict the effectiveness of slow uncrewed aircraft, while conventional surface-to-air missiles make deploying multi-million dollar combat aircraft against tactical targets economically unsustainable. Apollyon addresses the tactical gap between short-range commercial drones and expensive cruise missiles:

02 · The budget

Budget 2026 and long-term domestic spending

India has historically been among the world's largest arms importers despite maintaining a substantial defence budget. That dynamic is changing under government self-reliance policies. The Union Budget 2026 established the largest domestic capital allocation to date:

03 · Demand

Three independent demand pillars

A frequent vulnerability for defence contractors is customer concentration. Relying almost entirely on a single procurement authority leaves a business exposed to budget reallocations, committee delays, and shifting audit timelines. Apollyon addresses this risk by building for three distinct customer groups:

Vector 01 · Cross-service

Tri-service platform deployment

Apollyon avoids depending on any single military branch. Nightshade serves the Indian Army as a strike effector and all three services as an air-defence training target; Ahuti provides counter-UAS defence for Army units; Piranha is developed for Indian Navy and Coast Guard littoral operations; and Hemlock is designed for deep-strike interdiction with the Indian Air Force. Ahuti also addresses border-security requirements for the BSF and CRPF.

Vector 02 · Critical infra

Commercial & infrastructure protection

Demand for kinetic interceptors also extends beyond military services. Operators of critical industrial sites—including oil refineries, LNG terminals, offshore energy facilities, major seaports, and power stations—face direct threats from commercial and reconnaissance drones. Because low-cost drones can disrupt operations at multi-billion dollar facilities, industrial operators require automated perimeter defence. Ahuti provides an autonomous kinetic interceptor directly to infrastructure operators.

Vector 03 · Foreign markets

International defence export

Export sales decouple company revenue from domestic procurement cycles. Friendly foreign militaries across Asia, Eastern Europe, and the Middle East follow independent acquisition schedules, providing steady year-round production demand.

04 · Provenance

Weapons carry the foreign policy of the country that made them.

Western systems often carry ITAR export restrictions and complex licensing requirements, while Russian systems face international banking sanctions and supply shortages. An Indian, non-ITAR architecture provides friendly nations with a dependable alternative. Partnering with Tonbo Imaging, whose electro-optical systems are already active across 24 countries, gives Apollyon a direct pathway into non-aligned international markets.

05 · Exports

Building export volume outside ITAR

Export orders add volume on the same production line, spreading tooling, qualification, and development costs across more units. Western systems subject to US International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and Export Administration Regulations (EAR) often face strict congressional review, third-party transfer limits, and lengthy licensing cycles. Apollyon selects components to keep the production line outside ITAR. Following established Indian defence export precedents like the BrahMos cruise missile ($375M deal to the Philippines) and Akash air defence systems (₹6,000 Cr deal to Armenia), an Indian, non-ITAR supply chain offers international buyers a dependable option with predictable delivery schedules.
