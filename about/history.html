Hostel room to frontline

Apollyon Dynamics was founded by two twenty-year-olds at BITS Pilani, Hyderabad. The tools were minimal, the timeline was brutal, and the target customer was the Indian Army. Most people would have called it reckless. They called it a starting point.

01 · Origin

Built where they slept

Jayant Khatri and Sourya Choudhury did what most engineers only talk about: they cleared the bunk beds, moved in the machines, and got to work. The room smelled of solder and ambition in roughly equal measure.

At the age when most engineers are still choosing a first job, they were already putting hardware on the frontline. No defence startup accelerator, no government scheme, no years of procurement bureaucracy — just a working product and the credibility it earned. Within two months of formally founding the company they were shipping to active units. The hostel lab became a proper office, still on campus, still small, still moving faster than anyone expected.

02 · The gamble

Proof, not pitch decks

They had no connections in the defence establishment, no introductions, no warm referrals. What they had was a working product and a LinkedIn search bar. So they started writing to colonels, and they waited.

One colonel wrote back. They packed the hardware and drove to Chandigarh, where they put it all on the line in front of people who had seen a hundred pitches and been let down by most of them. The drones flew. The room got quiet. That quiet was the beginning of everything.

05 · Market

Problem statement and addressable market

Modern air defences and electronic warfare have made conventional manned strike missions unviable — and slow drones irrelevant. Three numbers frame the opportunity the company was founded against:

$70M+
Cost of a single manned combat aircraft sortie asset; a jet UAV replaces the same mission profile at a fraction of it (IISS Military Balance, 2024)
$11M+
Cost to train a single fighter pilot from undergraduate flight school through combat-ready qualification on 5th-gen platforms (RAND / US Air Force, 2024)
36:1
Cost asymmetry — defenders spend $4M+ per interceptor missile against systems costing a fraction; jet UAVs exploit this imbalance at scale
