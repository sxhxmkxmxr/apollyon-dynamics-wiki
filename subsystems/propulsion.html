Propulsion and the thrust margin that buys manoeuvre

Propulsion dictates speed, operating radius, and terminal manoeuvre authority. Sizing an engine for sea-level conditions creates severe deficits at high-altitude launch floors, leaving insufficient kinetic energy to complete steep terminal dives after long cruise sorties.

02 · The governing physics

High-altitude thrust derate

Turbine mass airflow decreases in direct proportion to ambient air density. At 4,500 metres—the operational terrain floor across Eastern Ladakh and North Sikkim—standard atmospheric density drops to 0.771 kg/m³, or 62.9% of sea-level density (1.225 kg/m³). A turbine sized with minimal margin at sea level loses 38% to 40% of its static thrust on the Himalayan plateau.

The design rule: size for the launch floor

Engine sizing must match the highest launch elevation rather than sea level. An airframe matched to sea-level thrust stalls in high-altitude mountain passes. Underpowered vehicles cannot climb over ridgelines, reject turbulence, or pull the g-loads required for terminal dives once cruise fuel is spent.

Lower air density at 4,500 metres also cuts wing lift. Airframes operating at plateau altitudes require larger surface areas, face reduced manoeuvre margins, and deliver shorter range than sea-level specifications suggest. High-altitude physics forms a primary design constraint across our entire strike fleet.

03 · Sovereignty

Sovereign turbine production versus salvage airframes

Apollyon rejects designing strike systems around salvaged civilian business-jet or trainer engines. Surplus Soviet AI-25 engines supported Ukraine's FP-5 Flamingo because thousands of airframes sat immediately in domestic warehouses during wartime. That approach served a specific operational context.

However, salvaged supply chains inevitably create production ceilings. By 2026, stockpile depletion became the binding constraint on Flamingo manufacturing, limiting deliveries to three airframes daily against a target of seven. Developing a replacement turbojet delayed serial production into 2027.

For India, salvaged commercial engines provide no reliable production base, trigger MTCR Category I export hurdles due to airframe mass, and bypass domestic engineering capability. Apollyon builds dedicated tooling for series-produced domestic turbines.

04 · Industrial base

Domestic manufacturing partnerships

  • GTRE and Azad Engineering: Gas Turbine Research Establishment transferred design packages for the 350 kgf expendable turbojet to Azad Engineering on 22 July 2026. Azad provides multi-axis CNC milling, blisk machining, and dynamic rotor balancing.
  • Godrej Aerospace: Fabricates high-tolerance fuel delivery components and precision turbine nozzle rings.
  • Bharat Forge and Aerolloy: Forges raw Inconel and nickel superalloy billets for the hot-section turbine wheel.

At volume production, the 350 kgf engine accounts for roughly 28% of flyaway cost—₹45–50 lakh within a ₹1.5–1.8 crore airframe. A smaller 40–100 kgf turbojet family supports our lighter loitering effectors.
