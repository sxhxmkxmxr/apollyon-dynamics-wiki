Seekers and terminal guidance

Modular seeker payloads separate airframe manufacturing from target engagement physics. The seeker determines whether an effector impacts a geographic coordinate or destroys a dynamic target, eliminating satellite navigation dependencies during the terminal engagement window.

01 · The family

Three terminal guidance modalities

Seeker register
SeekerPrincipleMissionTypical targets
EO / IR imaging Passive electro-optical and infrared imaging with terminal lock-on, independent of GNSS Precision strike on static and relocatable point targets Command posts · ammunition depots · critical infrastructure
Anti-radiation homing Passive homing on hostile radar emissions SEAD — suppression of enemy air defences, opening corridors for follow-on forces Search and track radars · SAM batteries · EW emitters
Scene-matching terminal lock Dual uncooled LWIR / visible automatic scene matcher, geometric silhouette match Steep terminal dive onto hardened structures Revetments · bridge piers · bunker portals

All three seeker variants operate completely passively. They emit zero radio frequency energy, preventing hostile electronic support measures from detecting the inbound weapon. This passive operating requirement mirrors the low-duty-cycle altimeter constraint detailed in GNSS-denied navigation.

02 · EO / IR

Tonbo TRAP-1 electro-optical and infrared seeker integration

The Tonbo TRAP-1 imaging seeker provides day-and-night target acquisition and terminal tracking without external satellite signals. Fast airframes collapse defender reaction times, but terminal accuracy determines target neutralization. Without terminal visual tracking, a fast missile simply arrives rapidly at an approximate coordinate.

Against mobile or relocatable targets, satellite coordinates become obsolete during flight transit. Even without jamming, coordinate guidance delivers rounds to vacant positions. The TRAP-1 resolves target visual features directly, tracking vehicle hulls and structural components up to impact.

Kinematics and terminal sensing

Speed secures ingress survivability; terminal sensing guarantees target neutralization. Apollyon couples high subsonic airframes with terminal imaging seekers because an unguided fast weapon and an intercepted slow drone produce the same operational failure.

03 · Anti-radiation

Passive anti-radiation homing

The anti-radiation seeker tracks radio frequency emissions from hostile air defence radars and electronic countermeasure systems. Its operational role is air-defence suppression, compelling hostile surface-to-air missile batteries to deactivate their emitters or face terminal strikes.

This payload enables layered strike doctrine. Inexpensive attritable effectors fly ahead of heavy cruise missiles, forcing hostile batteries to illuminate and deplete missile magazines before main strike packages enter the sector. See The missing middle.

04 · Scene matching

Terminal scene matching and silhouette lock

On heavy strike rounds like the Hemlock family, terminal guidance uses a dual uncooled LWIR and visible automatic scene matcher. At the terminal waypoint, the weapon transitions into a steep high-angle terminal dive profile, matching ground silhouettes against onboard geometric models to achieve a circular error probable under 1.5 metres.

We select uncooled microbolometers deliberately. Cooled thermal detectors offer higher raw sensitivity but require cryogenic pumps, high electrical power, and lengthy cooldown cycles. Onboard neural models match structural edge geometry rather than absolute thermal gradients, so night-time infrared frames align against pre-stored daylight satellite tiles.
