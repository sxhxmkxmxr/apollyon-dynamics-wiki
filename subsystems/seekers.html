Seekers and terminal guidance

One airframe, swappable seekers. The seeker is the component that decides whether a weapon hits a coordinate or hits a target, and it is the last place in the kill chain where satellite navigation can be removed from the dependency list entirely.

01 · The family

Three terminal modes

Seeker register
SeekerPrincipleMissionTypical targets
EO / IR imaging Passive electro-optical and infrared imaging with terminal lock-on, independent of GNSS Precision strike on static and relocatable point targets Command posts · ammunition depots · critical infrastructure
Anti-radiation homing Passive homing on hostile radar emissions SEAD — suppression of enemy air defences, opening corridors for follow-on forces Search and track radars · SAM batteries · EW emitters
Scene-matching terminal lock Dual uncooled LWIR / visible automatic scene matcher, geometric silhouette match Steep terminal dive onto hardened structures Revetments · bridge piers · bunker portals

All three are passive. Using them does not announce the vehicle, which matters because anything that transmits can be heard — the same constraint that governs radar-altimeter duty cycle in GNSS-denied navigation.

02 · EO / IR

The Tonbo Trap imaging seeker

A passive EO/IR imaging seeker providing all-weather, day and night target acquisition and terminal lock, independent of satellite navigation and resistant to electronic warfare. This is the component that converts a fast airframe into a precision weapon: speed collapses the defender's engagement window, but without an imaging terminal phase the round arrives quickly at an approximate place.

Against small or relocatable targets, a baseline GNSS + INS guidance stack gives poor terminal precision even when it is not being jammed, because the target's coordinate at launch is not its coordinate at arrival. An imaging seeker resolves the target itself rather than a position.

Why this is the pairing that matters

Speed buys survivability; precision buys effect. The company's design position is that a strike system must have both, because a fast weapon that misses and a precise weapon that is intercepted are the same weapon from the target's point of view.

03 · Anti-radiation

Passive anti-radiation homing

A passive anti-radiation seeker homes on hostile radar emissions to hunt and neutralise air-defence radars and SAM systems. Its doctrinal purpose is corridor opening: forcing an integrated air-defence network either to radiate and be attacked, or to stay silent and stop defending.

This is the seeker that makes the cheap-rounds-first firing order work. Attritable rounds sent first do two jobs at once — they service soft targets and they force the defender to spend interceptors and radiate — so that heavier rounds arrive against a partly empty magazine. See The missing middle.

04 · Scene matching

Terminal scene matching and silhouette lock

On the heavy round the terminal sensor is a dual uncooled LWIR / visible automatic scene matcher. At the terminal waypoint the vehicle pitches up, resolves the target silhouette against a stored geometric reference, and plunges at 80 degrees. Accuracy is specified at CEP under 1.5 m.

Uncooled sensors are chosen deliberately: a cooled detector would give better sensitivity but brings a cryogenic cooler, its power draw, its warm-up time and its cost onto an expendable vehicle. The learned matching layer recovers the performance that a cheaper detector gives up — the model is asked to describe permanent structural geometry rather than to compare brightness, which is what makes an uncooled thermal frame matchable against public daylight imagery.
