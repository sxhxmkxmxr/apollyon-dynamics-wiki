Airframe and structures

Structure is where the manufacturability of a weapon is decided. A design that is two percent lighter and needs an autoclave slot is a worse design than one that is two percent heavier and can be produced by a tier-1 automotive supplier who already exists.

01 · Principle

Design for the supply base that exists

Missile programmes fail when they are designed around components that cannot be manufactured domestically at rate. Every structural decision in the portfolio is checked against a single question: can an existing Indian precision or automotive supplier build this part, at the required rate, without a two-year-lead-time import?

India holds an enormous civilian manufacturing base in exactly the disciplines a cheap-mass strike vehicle needs — automotive stamping, castings, precision machining, electronics and composites. The structural architecture is drawn deliberately onto that base rather than onto a notional aerospace one.

03 · Specifics

Three structural decisions and what they buy

Out-of-autoclave resin transfer moulding

Autoclave capacity is one of the hardest bottlenecks in aerospace composite production and it cannot be scaled quickly. Moving the skins to out-of-autoclave RTM removes that constraint from the production rate equation entirely, which is what makes 80–120 rounds a month a credible number rather than an aspiration.

Stamped bulkheads and automotive actuators

Stamped aluminium bulkheads and brushless DC servo actuators derived from commercial automotive steer-by-wire suppliers, rated for the 120-minute operational life of an expendable vehicle. An expendable weapon does not need a 30,000-hour actuator, and specifying one imports cost and lead time for no operational return.

Rigidity as a control requirement

On the fast small airframes, structural rigidity is a guidance parameter, not only a strength one. Structural vibration cross-talk is one of the terms the physics backbone has to carry, because airframe flex pollutes the inertial measurement that the control policy depends on. The smart-infill structure on Ahuti is chosen for stiffness per unit mass for exactly this reason.

The signature consequence

Signature reduction on these vehicles is passive and geometric — a chined nose, a dorsal S-duct masking the engine face, and a moulded low-drag body — rather than applied treatment. Geometry is free at rate; treatment is not. Frontal RCS under 0.03–0.05 m² in X-band is achieved by shape.
