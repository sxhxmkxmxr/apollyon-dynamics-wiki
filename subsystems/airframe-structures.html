Airframe and structures

Airframe architecture dictates manufacturing throughput. A structure that is two percent lighter but demands autoclave curing creates production bottlenecks. Apollyon designs airframes for out-of-autoclave resin transfer moulding and high-volume automotive stamping lines.

01 · Principle

Designing for existing domestic industrial capacity

Missile production stalls when airframe designs require specialized tooling unavailable at scale. Every structural choice in our portfolio satisfies a practical test: can an established Indian automotive or precision machining supplier manufacture this component at rate without two-year machine tool lead times?

India maintains extensive commercial manufacturing infrastructure suited for attritable strike systems: automated metal stamping, aluminum casting, multi-axis CNC machining, electronics assembly, and commercial composites. Apollyon routes structural production through this established tier-1 industrial base rather than waiting for dedicated defence aerospace lines.

03 · Specifics

Three structural decisions and their operational returns

Out-of-autoclave resin transfer moulding

Autoclaves represent capital-intensive bottlenecks that cap composite production rates. Transitioning fuselage shells to out-of-autoclave resin transfer moulding (RTM) removes autoclave cycle times from the assembly schedule. This manufacturing choice enables sustained production runs of 80 to 120 rounds per month.

Stamped bulkheads and automotive steer-by-wire actuators

Internal bulkheads use progressive die aluminum stamping borrowed from automotive chassis manufacturing. Control surfaces rely on commercial brushless DC servos rated for the 120-minute mission duration of an expendable missile. Specifying 30,000-hour aerospace-grade actuators on one-way rounds inflates procurement costs and extends lead times without adding mission capability.

Structural rigidity as a guidance parameter

In high-speed airframes, structural rigidity directly influences guidance precision. Aeroelastic deflection introduces sensor vibration into inertial measurement units, corrupting high-rate state estimation. Our physics backbone models airframe bending modes, while optimized carbon infill maintains structural stiffness during high-g pull-ups.

Geometric signature management

Radar cross-section reduction relies on external geometry rather than maintenance-intensive radar-absorbent coatings. Chined nose chines, planar facets, and a dorsal flush S-duct concealing the turbine face yield frontal radar cross-sections below 0.03–0.05 m² in X-band. Molded geometry adds zero recurring cost on serial production lines.
