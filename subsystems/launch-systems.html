Launch and ground systems

A weapon's readiness time is a specification, not a logistics detail. An interceptor that needs ten minutes of preparation has already lost the engagement, and a strike round that needs a prepared site cannot be dispersed.

02 · Principles

Three requirements the ground segment has to meet

Zero-length rail and booster, for the heavy round

A turbojet cannot start a 650–750 kg vehicle from rest. A solid rocket booster of roughly 100–150 kg takes the round off a containerised zero-length rail and up to turbojet operating speed, then separates. The rail is containerised so that the launch unit is a sealed round on a standard 8×8 chassis rather than a prepared installation — which is what makes dispersal, and therefore survivability, possible.

Thirty minutes, all weather, wide temperature band

Nightshade is specified at 30 minutes or less from arrival to launch-ready, in all-weather day and night conditions, across an ambient band of −15 °C to +50 °C. The HACM-350 adds the high-altitude case: a launch floor of 4,000–5,500 m AMSL under a certified −30 °C to +55 °C band, per HQ IDS TPCR 2025 Line Item #15.

Near-zero preparation, for the interceptor

Ahuti's readiness requirement is qualitatively different because the engagement timeline is set by the threat, not by the operator. There is nothing to assemble, fuel or align. The round sits ready, the ground radar declares a track, and the vehicle launches. Midcourse guidance is radar-cued, so the interceptor does not have to search.

03 · Host platforms

Vehicles and command integration

Launchers are mounted on standard Indian Army high-mobility vehicles wherever possible, so that the ground segment inherits an existing maintenance, spares and driver-training base rather than creating a new one.

Where an MDCC is present in the sector, the air picture it holds is what cues the interceptors, and the assessments it produces are what nominate targets for the strike rounds. The ground segment is therefore not only a launch mechanism — it is the point where Cortex meets the effector.

Boundary, restated

Cortex explicitly excludes autonomous engagement and weapon control. It nominates and disseminates; the launch decision stays with the chain of command. See Precision is Mercy.
