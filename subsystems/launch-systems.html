Launch and ground systems

Readiness time functions as a core design specification. An interceptor that requires ten minutes of preparation misses its engagement window, while a strike missile that demands prepared launch pads cannot disperse across tactical terrain.

02 · Principles

Ground-segment engineering requirements

Zero-length canister rail and solid booster

An air-breathing turbojet cannot accelerate a 650–750 kg missile from zero airspeed. A 100–150 kg solid rocket booster accelerates the round off a zero-length rail, delivering the airframe to turbojet ignition speed within 2.5 seconds before cleanly jettisoning. Encapsulating the rail inside a sealed canister allows standard 8×8 military trucks to transport, erect, and fire rounds without fixed launch infrastructure.

Rapid deployment across extreme temperature envelopes

Nightshade deploys from transport cases to flight status in under 30 minutes across ambient temperatures from −15 °C to +50 °C. The HACM-350 extends this operating ceiling to Himalayan launch floors between 4,000 and 5,500 metres AMSL across a certified −30 °C to +55 °C band, meeting HQ IDS TPCR 2025 Line Item #15 requirements.

Immediate response for interceptor canisters

Ahuti's readiness constraint is dictated by incoming threat velocities rather than operator scheduling. The round requires zero field assembly, propellant servicing, or sensor alignment. Sealed canisters stand on ready alert; when sector radar establishes a track file, the interceptor launches automatically under radar guidance without preliminary visual search.

03 · Host platforms

Chassis compatibility and command links

Launch hardware mounts directly onto standard Indian Army high-mobility logistics vehicles. This integration leverages existing maintenance depots, spare parts inventories, and driver certifications instead of introducing proprietary transport platforms.

When an MDCC operates in the sector, its local air picture feeds tracking vectors directly to canister batteries, while its target nomination pipeline allocates coordinates to strike packages. Ground launchers connect directly with Cortex to receive mission assignments.

Command hierarchy boundary

Cortex does not execute autonomous weapon release. The software correlates tracks and nominates engagement solutions, while human commanders retain sole authority for launch decisions. See Precision is Mercy.
