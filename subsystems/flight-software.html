Flight Software Stack

Built on established open-source flight libraries (PX4, ArduPilot), re-engineered for natively autonomous strike weapons. The execution core provides direct-actuator neural control, microsecond deterministic scheduling, and continuous operation under high dynamic pressure.

01 · Foundation

Open-source baseline and peripheral abstraction

Open-source autopilots—chiefly ArduPilot and PX4—have accumulated millions of operating hours over fifteen years. They established durable abstractions for peripheral buses, sensor drivers, and ground communication protocols.

Apollyon preserves the mature lower-level components of this ecosystem:

  • Device Driver Abstraction: Tested bus communication drivers across SPI, I2C, CAN FD, and UART for standard sensor peripherals.
  • Standardized Telemetry: Compact binary telemetry framing based on established packet definitions for field diagnostics.
  • Peripheral Bringup: Rapid hardware interface bringup across new avionics revisions without writing custom hardware register drivers from scratch.

We keep these communication drivers. However, standard open-source flight stacks assume low velocities (<40 m/s), benign g-loads, and an active radio link to a human operator. In a high-subsonic strike weapon, those assumptions fail.

02 · Structural limitations

Why commercial drone firmware fails in strike weapons

Standard open-source flight stacks were designed around operational rules that directly conflict with high-speed autonomous strike requirements:

Core architectural divergences
DomainOpen-source firmware assumption (PX4 / ArduPilot)Autonomous high-speed strike weapon reality
Operator presence Assumes an RC transmitter, ground station, and human pilot with manual override modes (Manual, Acro, AltHold, RTL). Natively autonomous: Zero pilot in the loop, zero manual override, no RC link, and no return-to-launch logic from rail launch to impact.
Control architecture Decoupled cascaded PID loops (Position → Attitude → Angular Rate → Mixer) assuming linear dynamics below 40 m/s. Coupled non-linear dynamics: At Mach 0.8 (250+ m/s) and high dynamic pressure, cross-axis coupling requires direct-actuator neural control.
Actuator saturation Mixer sacrifices secondary axis authority when servo channels hit maximum deflection limits. Hard saturation operation: Weapon spends substantial terminal dive time on actuator rails; requires direct coupled torque allocation.
Scheduling & determinism Cooperative workqueue threads subject to 5–15 ms latency jitter and dynamic OS scheduling overhead. Microsecond hard real-time: 500 Hz direct control policies demand deterministic sub-millisecond execution boundaries.
High-G dynamics & shock EKF tuned for low-vibration quadcopters; resets or diverges under severe motor vibration or sustained 7G turns. High-G resilient estimation: Maintains state tracking through rocket booster staging, catapult launch, and 7G evasive maneuvers.
Autonomy increases flight software demands

Removing the human operator increases the burden on the onboard guidance computer. In a commercial drone, when an estimator drifts or a motor saturates, the software triggers a failsafe and prompts the human pilot to take manual control. In an expendable cruise missile or high-speed interceptor traveling at 250 m/s, no human intervention is possible. The software must resolve aerodynamic non-linearities autonomously and instantaneously.

03 · The re-engineered stack

What was kept, what was rewritten, and what was added

Apollyon retains the peripheral device drivers from the open-source community while replacing the control laws, state estimation, and scheduling layers with a deterministic execution core.

Layer-by-layer architectural breakdown
LayerStatusEngineering rationale
Sensor device driversKept & hardenedPhysical bus communication (SPI, I2C, CAN FD) operates reliably; retained and audited for memory safety.
High-G state estimationRewrittenReplaced with a tightly coupled UKF engineered to withstand booster shock, engine vibration, and sustained 7G turns without filter divergence.

Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) harnessAddedIntegrated digital-analog test harness executing production flight binaries against real-time aerodynamics simulation models.
04 · Verification harness

Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing pipeline

Software verification happens on the ground. Every firmware build undergoes thousands of simulated sorties inside our Hardware-in-the-Loop test bench before reaching the launch rail:

  • Production Binaries: The flight computer on the HIL test bench executes the exact, bit-for-bit compiled binary deployed to the field. No simulation flags or altered timing paths are permitted.
  • Full Sortie Emulation: The HIL test bench injects synthetic IMU signals, barometric pressure profiles, radar altimeter returns, and optical scene frames, validating complete 800-kilometre missions from booster separation to terminal dive.
  • Automated Fault Injection: The test harness injects jammed control surfaces, motor failures, electronic warfare RF interference, and sensor dropouts, ensuring the flight software executes robust fail-operational contingencies.
05 · Ownership thesis

Why owning the execution core compounds development velocity

Airframe structural components can be procured through domestic manufacturing partners, but flight control authority cannot. Third-party software stacks impose structural compromises: conservative flight limits, non-deterministic latency jitter, and architectures designed around human operators.
